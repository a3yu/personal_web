# Is it Midjourney’s Chegg Moment?

**By: Aedin Yu**

Chegg needs no introduction. If you were a student during the pandemic, the name was synonymous with easy answers—a digital lifeline when midnight deadlines loomed and the textbook made no sense. From a business perspective, Chegg was an edtech darling, enjoying a meteoric rise during the pandemic and reaching a $12 billion market cap at its peak. While a post-COVID cooldown was expected, the company still appeared well-positioned, with strong brand recognition, a colossal user base, and the potential to evolve beyond just helping students cheat on their Zoom exams.

ChatGPT 3.5 launched on November 30, 2022. With it came the frontier of artificial intelligence, abstracted into a chat window accessible to everyone. While the use cases of such a technology were still coming into focus, the tool quickly gained viral traction among students who realized they could use it for assignment help.

The story so far plays out exactly as you’d expect: Chegg plummeted from a $12 billion to a $65 million market cap. Now, when you need academic help, you pull up ChatGPT, and the thought of Chegg only surfaces when reminiscing about quarantine. The logic seems straightforward: ChatGPT emerged as the more capable and broadly accessible solution, rendering Chegg obsolete.

OpenAI recently released GPT-4o’s image generation, and from the eye test, it’s incredible—in my opinion, it outperforms most, if not all, current AI image-gen tools. People on X are calling this the death of all image generation companies. So, is this their Chegg moment?

I’d give it a **30% chance**. I’m definitely not as doomer as many on X, but I’m also not delusional. I theorize that the user bases of Midjourney and ChatGPT meaningfully intersect, and with GPT-4o’s image generation appearing to surpass the current state of the art, Midjourney could face significant user churn. But I’m not completely bearish. Here are my back-of-the-napkin thoughts:

### Network effects

Midjourney’s “Explore” page is genuinely compelling—and something I don’t think OpenAI can pull off without overcomplicating its core product. A feed of random or “hot” AI-generated images doesn’t provide much value in itself. But a well-executed style repository or other social features that enhance media generation could serve as a strong and defensible moat. This kind of social scaffolding creates a self-reinforcing loop: better outputs attract more users, which in turn surfaces more valuable styles and use cases for others to remix and build on.

### GTM for AI-illiterate users

There’s likely some of this already happening, but it’s still worth emphasizing—AI-illiterate users are far less likely to churn just because a new frontier model drops. That stickiness gives you time to build. That said, I wouldn’t bet on this lasting. As AI literacy increases across the general population, consumer decision-making will evolve—and this moat will erode. Still, it’s a valuable early advantage that can be capitalized on in the short term.

### Outcompete on the model layer

This is always on the table—and it’d be naive (and frankly very un–Silicon Valley) to rule it out. I don’t know anyone at Midjourney, but dismissing the possibility that they could eventually build a model on par with or better than frontier labs feels shortsighted. Betting against them purely on model quality alone is a weak position.

### Abstract, abstract, abstract

Doubling down on abstractions—interfaces that pull users away from the raw act of prompt engineering and closer to simply “thinking” and expressing ideas—is hugely powerful. I love what Pika Labs is doing with Pikaffects for exactly this reason. It creates several advantages:

1. It makes you less dependent on your own model’s frontier performance (you can commoditize the model layer as needed)
2. It creates opportunities for viral, differentiated product marketing via novel features and effects
3. It gives you a pathway to compete with incumbents like Photoshop by appealing to non-technical users who just want to create

Done right, abstraction is both a product unlock and a distribution unlock.

---

I’m optimistic that with the right product choices, Midjourney can stick around and isn’t nearly as doomed as people on X think. Watching this play out will likely become a case study for many other industries like voice gen and video gen.

**Excited to see what happens.**
